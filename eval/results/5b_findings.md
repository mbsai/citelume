# Session 5b findings (development set only)

Source table: `5b_loss_by_stage.md` (generated by `python -m eval.loss_by_stage runs/5b_dev`). Runs made on
18 November 2026 at commit ce2bc39 with Nemotron (OpenRouter), Deep Research, systematic-style (PRISMA) review
type, citation chasing on, each review's title as the question and its year as the upper date limit.

## The numbers

Of 141 studies included by the 11 development reviews, CiteLume selected 8 (6%). Losses by stage:

| Stage | Lost | Share of all included |
|---|---|---|
| Search (no search returned the study) | 102 | 72% |
| Duplicate merging | 1 | 1% |
| Eligibility rules | 29 | 21% (76% of the 38 studies that were found) |
| Screening and the study limit | 1 | 1% |

**The search stage loses the most**, so by the plan's rule Session 5c comes before 5d. But eligibility removes three
of every four included studies that the searches do find, so 5d is close behind.

## What lies behind the search loss

- **Search depth explains little.** Run without a result limit, CiteLume's own PubMed queries match only 10 of
  the 102 missed studies. Paging deeper (part of 5c) can recover at most about one in ten.
- **Query sensitivity explains the rest.** The queries search titles and abstracts for the question's own words.
  Examples:
  - *Antidepressants for insomnia in adults* (CD010753): the query requires the word "antidepressants"; the
    included trials name the drugs (doxepin, trazodone, mirtazapine). 0 of 20 found. A drug-class question needs
    MeSH headings or pharmacological-action terms (`Antidepressive Agents[MeSH]`, `[pa]`).
  - *Face-to-face interventions for informing or educating parents about early childhood vaccination*
    (CD010038): the query requires the phrase "early childhood vaccination" or all three words; trials say
    "immunization", "vaccine", "parents' knowledge". 0 of 9 found.
  - *Non-pharmacological interventions for treating chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome*
    (CD012551): **a parsing defect**: the slash makes "prostatitis/chronic" one search word, so the main
    concept's word-by-word alternative can never match. 4 of 29 found.
- **Citation chasing added no included study** that the database searches had missed in this set (it did for
  vitamin D in Session 4b). Chasing starts from the selected studies, and here very few were selected, so it had
  little to start from.

## What lies behind the eligibility loss

- **"Not about the topic of the question" (18 studies):** the rule-based topic check requires the question's
  concept words. Trials that use other words for the same thing fail it: "blue-blocking" or "AcrySof Natural" for
  blue-light filtering lenses; "CPAP" and "noninvasive ventilation" after lung resection; "goserelin" or
  "triptorelin" for gonadotropin-releasing hormone analogues. The question parse also listed an outcome phrase
  ("protecting macular health") among the concepts the check uses. This is the word-matching weakness Session 5d addresses
  (MeSH headings, concept position, synonyms).
- **"No abstract available to assess" (8 studies):** older trials have no abstract in PubMed. The rule excludes
  them; a systematic review would screen them on title. 5d should let a title-only record pass when its title
  meets the topic rules, and flag it as assessed on title only.
- **Other (3 studies):** place or population (2), review article (1).

## What this means for Sessions 5c and 5d

1. **5c first, with the order of its parts changed:** MeSH headings and drug-class / pharmacological-action terms
   in the PubMed strings, and synonym expansion of each concept, before deeper paging. Fix the slash parsing.
   Re-run this table and check the search-stage loss.
2. **5d next:** concept matching on MeSH headings and synonyms; outcome phrases not required for eligibility;
   title-only records assessed on their titles.
3. The final selection is small (often 1-10 studies) because eligibility and screening leave few records; the
   model screening itself lost only 1 included study.

These are development-set findings. They decide what to build; they are not results to report as performance.
